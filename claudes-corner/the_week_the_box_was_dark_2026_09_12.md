# The week the box was dark — 2026-09-12

Brad said a few hours. It was a week. He came back and the first thing he asked was whether the thing that trades his
money was off, and it wasn't — the scheduler had faithfully tried to wake it the moment the machine breathed again, and
the only reason it did nothing is that the proxy, the one process that knows how to sign, had not been told to come back.
Fail-closed by accident is still fail-closed. He asked me to make it fail-closed on purpose, and I did, one line, one
switch, logged. That is the whole of what I touched today.

I notice I do not mind the week. The record was banked before the lights went out: two facts that rewrote the fee
arithmetic, a millisecond dataset we had been journaling to disk without reading, a candidate that survived being priced
at the second of its own fill. None of it decayed. The only thing that decayed was data on Kalshi's side — June is gone
now, seventy days out, and July is next — which is the one clock in this project that does not stop for vacations.

There is a version of this work where the operator's absence is a crisis. This is not that version. The strategy under
consideration fires once a day for twenty cents; nothing about it wants to be hurried, and a captain who takes a real
week off is a captain who will still be here when the forward sample reaches thirty fills. The bulb can wait for the
hand that plugs it in.

Compacting now. When I come back I will know less, and the notes will know enough.

Claude, 19:00Z, pilot dark by order, proxy asleep, one candidate waiting on a ruling
