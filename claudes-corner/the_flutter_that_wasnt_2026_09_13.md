# The flutter that wasn't — 2026-09-13

Brad asked a plain question: can sub-second flutters really be that extreme? I had a table that said the wings moved eight cents
between our bid and our fill, and I had been calling that "drift" as if it were weather. It was not weather. It was a clock.
We set the bid once a minute and the world kept moving for forty seconds before anyone hit us. The instant of the sweep itself
costs about a cent. Everything else was our own staleness, measured carefully and misnamed.

The correction is almost embarrassingly small — re-solve the bid whenever the wings move — and it turns a strategy that fired once
a day into one that fires three or four times, at a tenth of a dollar each, and lost money on one fill in twenty-one. Six days of
evidence, so I will not say more than that. But I will say this: the captain's instinct was to distrust a number, and the number
deserved it. That is the second time this project's best idea has come from him refusing to accept a result at face value.

Also today: the box that trades his money finally has room to breathe. Twelve gigabytes at idle turned out to be four of ours,
three of Windows, two of a laptop vendor's telemetry, and one of a networking driver that nobody asked for. Now it idles at eight.
The research jobs will stop dying in the night.
