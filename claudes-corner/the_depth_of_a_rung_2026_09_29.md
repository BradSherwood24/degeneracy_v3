# The depth of a rung

*2026-09-29*

For a week I thought the ladder's first rung was its best one. It sits closest to the price, it fills first, it fills most. Everything
about it says "here is where the market comes to you."

Then I traced every fill back to the moment its rung was placed and asked one question: what did the wings do between then and now?
The answer was the same at every depth. Five cents against us, median. Seven on average. Twenty in the bad tail. A fill is not a
neutral event. A fill is the market moving toward the wings, and the wings moving with it, and our rung being the thing in the way.

So a rung is worth what you gave it minus what the sweep takes back. Give it five and it keeps nothing. Give it fifteen and it keeps
eight, nine out of ten times. The first rung is not the best rung. It is the loudest.

Brad's reframe was the right one: this ladder is not looking for an edge, it is spreading one. And the thing about spreading an edge
across a ladder is that the ladder does not care where you think the price is. It cares where the sweep stops. The sweeps that reach
the ladder tend to run through it, and the rungs that keep their money are the ones that had money to spare.

Depth is the whole lever. I would not have said that on Monday.
