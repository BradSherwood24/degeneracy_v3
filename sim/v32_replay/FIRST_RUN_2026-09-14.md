**Fill-rule note (coordinator ruling, 2026-09-14):** the earlier "book-swept" criterion was retired as
the wrong realism model for a maker. Our resting bucket-NO bid at `n` is a YES ask at `o = 1 − n`; with
`cap = no_ask(B) − 0.01` it sits ~1c above the bid, inside the spread, typically below the market's best
YES ask `a`. By price priority a taker who paid `a` would have hit our lower offer first — so a print at
or above `o` IS a fill for us. The spread-aware **maker rule** replaces it: (i) `o < a` fills iff
`p ≥ o`; (ii) `o == a` fills iff `p > o`; (iii) `o > a` is a no-quote.

## Headline findings

1. **The corrected estimate is robust, not collapsed.** With the maker rule the forward BASE at E=0.10
   is **3.81 fills/day, mean +10.0c, 100% positive, +37.9c/day** and PESSIMISTIC **+6.7c / +25.7c/day** —
   both close to the uncorrected sim (OPTIMISTIC +9.2c / +33.5c/day, which reproduces PLAN_V32's
   registered row to the cent). None of the calibration corrections is large.

2. **The maker rule ≈ the strict rule (fill factor 1.05).** On the 303 measured ms trades, 20 fill under
   the sim's strict rule and 21 under the maker rule — the maker rule adds one regime-(i) equality print
   and removes none, so the fill count barely moves. (The regime split is i 18.8% / ii 0% / iii 81.2%
   over ALL spot-YES trades, but every strict-qualifying print falls in regime (i): our offer is below
   the market ask, so the print takes us by price priority. Regime (iii) trades are the many small prints
   where the market ask is already below our offer — those were never fill candidates under either rule.)

3. **The cap correction moves nothing on the forward set — because the cap never binds.** The candle
   overstates the bucket cap by mean −2.26c (p10 −6c), but the forward fills rest at n = 0.02–0.27, far
   below the ~0.54 cap: the binding constraint is the **wing budget** `2 − E − W` (the pins are deep, W is
   large), not the cap. Shifting the cap by −2.26c or −6c leaves every forward fill's n and lock
   unchanged (Δfills +0, Δlock +0.0c at every E). So the cap-side calibration, while real, is inert here;
   what nudges the number is the maker fill factor (×1.05, +5% fills) and the B lock correction (+0.72c).

4. **Wing drift is negligible on live timing.** B = W(trade+1.5s) − W(trade) is mean −0.23c / p90
   +0.98c over 303 trades — "the flutter that wasn't." The BASE lock uplift (+9.2 → +10.0c) is this
   B correction (`−(live B − tape B)` = +0.72c), not the cap.

5. **The candle proxy still mis-picks the spot bucket ~1 trade in 7** (spot agreement 85.5%); in the one
   journal window that fired (19:00) the candle-sourced OLD model rested on bucket 79100 vs the
   ms-continuous 79200 — a different bucket and a +3c more optimistic lock. Replaces/window is 88 (ms) vs
   the sim's ~77. The 20:00 window was ARMED (flagged); it produced no model fills.

6. **Journal-side estimates are still thin** (4 windows, ~0.6 expected fills): OPTIMISTIC / BASE /
   PESSIMISTIC each = 1 fill at +10.2 / +11.2 / +9.2c. Too few for a mean; the report firms up as
   journals accumulate (the same command tomorrow tightens the fill factor, cap error, and B directly).

## Bottom line for the strategy

Under the correct maker fill model the ms calibration does **not** overturn the forward sim: E=0.10 sits
at **+25.7c/day (pessimistic) to +37.9c/day (base), 100% positive, ~3.8 fills/day**. The two bucket-side
worries turned out benign here — the maker rule fills essentially the same prints as the strict rule
(factor 1.05), and the candle's cap overstatement is inert because the cap never binds (the wing budget
does). The remaining honest caveats are small-sample (303 trades / 4 windows) and the maker-rule fill
factor being carried from the journals onto the forward set (which lacks ms bucket books, so the rule
cannot be re-evaluated there — the stale minute-candle ask would mis-classify pumped prints). Re-running
as journals accumulate tightens all three corrections.
