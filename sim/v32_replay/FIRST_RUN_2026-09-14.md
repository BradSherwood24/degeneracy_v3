## Headline findings

1. **The forward apply reproduces the sim to the cent.** OPTIMISTIC (uncorrected) at E=0.10 over 139 h:
   **21 fills, 3.63/day, mean +9.2c, p10 +5.5c, 100% positive, +33.5c/day** — identical to PLAN_V32's
   registered E=10/TOL=2/DEB=5000 row. E=0.08 → 24 fills/+7.6c; E=0.12 → 15 fills/+11.1c. The port is
   faithful, so the corrections below are the only thing that moves the number.

2. **The decisive calibration result: `P(swept | print rule) = 0 of 20`.** Across the four ms windows,
   303 spot-bucket YES trades were measured; 20 printed above our offer (1 − n, the sim's fill rule);
   **none** of those 20 had the bucket's best YES ask already at/below our offer. Our resting offer sits
   ~1c above the bid, INSIDE the spread (n is cap-limited at ≈ bid+1c → offer ≈ 1 − cap ≈ bid+1c), while
   the real best YES ask is ~spread (2–3c) higher. So the sim's print-rule fills are **counterfactual
   "we'd be the best ask" fills, not demonstrated sweeps of existing depth.** Applying P(swept) as a
   thinning factor drives the corrected forward BASE/PESSIMISTIC **fill rate to ≈ 0** (×0.000).
   *Caveat — small sample:* 0/20 is a bound, not a hard zero. Rule of three gives P(swept) ≲ 15% at 95%
   confidence (n=20). Read it as "provisionally near-zero, and the sweep-realism haircut is severe,"
   pending more windows. This is exactly the assumption the lab exists to firm up.

3. **The candle proxy mis-prices the bucket side.** Spot-bucket agreement (candle vs ms) is **85.5%** —
   the minute-candle picks the wrong spot bucket ~1 trade in 7. Cap error `cap_ms − cap_candle` is
   **mean −2.26c (p10 −6c, p90 +2c)**: the candle *overstates* the cap ~2c on average, so the
   uncorrected sim rests at a slightly-too-high n. The one window that fired (19:00) shows it live: the
   candle-sourced OLD model rested on bucket 79100 while the ms-continuous model rested on 79200 — a
   different bucket and a +3c higher (optimistic) lock (14.2c vs 11.2c).

4. **Wing drift is negligible on live timing.** B = W(trade+1.5s) − W(trade) is **mean −0.23c, p90
   +0.98c** over 303 trades — confirming "the flutter that wasn't"; the +1.5s completion barely moves
   the wings. Requote cadence is **88 replaces/window** vs the sim's ~77 (close; ms ticks a touch more).

5. **Journal-side estimates are still thin.** Only the 19:00 window crossed an offer; across the whole
   grid all 31 model fills sit in that one hour, and **all are non-book-swept**. OPTIMISTIC (ideal,
   E=0.10) = 1 fill over 4 windows, +10.2c; BASE / PESSIMISTIC (book-swept only) = 0. Four windows is
   ~0.6 of an expected fill at 3.6/day — too few for a mean. The report firms up as journals accumulate.

6. **The 20:00 window was ARMED** (the live-order switch was on) and is flagged in the tables; it
   produced no model fills (no spot-bucket YES print crossed the offer). The fill rule uses only public
   bucket trades; our own fills would arrive as private `fill` frames, which the lab does not consume.

## Bottom line for the strategy

The uncorrected forward sim says +33.5c/day at E=0.10. The ms calibration attaches two warnings to that
number: the bucket cap the sim reads off the candle is ~2c too generous (a small, correctable bias — it
actually *raises* the corrected per-fill lock to +10.0c), and — the load-bearing one — the print-rule
fills are essentially never demonstrated sweeps of resting depth at our price (0/20). Whether the
"book-swept" filter is the right realism model for a *maker* (who would itself be resting at that price,
adding the depth that gets swept) is a judgment call for Brad; either way the calibration says the sim's
fill assumption is the fragile part, and 20 qualifying prints is too few to settle it. Re-running this
command as journals accumulate tightens P(swept), the cap error, and the B residual directly.
